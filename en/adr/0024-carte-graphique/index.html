ADR 0024: A graphics card handed to the applications VM

An entry-level GeForce is handed whole to the applications VM (PCI passthrough) for photo recognition and video conversion, with a driver maintained by Debian and containers that request it through CDI.

19 October 2026 accepted #hardware#proxmox#immich#gpu 3 min read

Context

The server’s processor (Xeon E3-1220 v6, 4 cores) has no graphics unit. All the heavy work of the applications went through it:

  • photo recognition (faces, search by description);
  • converting videos for phones and browsers.

During imports (about 11,500 photos in October), the applications VM stayed saturated for hours and the rest of the server suffered. A GeForce GTX 1050 (Pascal, 2 GB, 75 W, no extra power connector) was added to the server on 19/10.

Options considered

  1. Stay on the processor: no change, but imports and conversions stay just as long.
  2. Hand the whole card to the applications VM (PCI passthrough, vfio): native performance, driver inside the VM; only one VM can use it.
  3. Driver on the host, shared with LXC containers: goes against the one-VM-per-security-zone choice (ADR 0010), and the host would run the proprietary driver.
  4. Share the card between several VMs (vGPU): not available on a GeForce.

Decision

Option 2, on the applications VM, where all the services that benefit from it run.

  • Host: the card is reserved for vfio-pci from boot (host drivers kept away) and declared as a Proxmox “mapping”. OpenTofu’s token only has rights on that mapping, not on the rest of the hardware.
  • VM: the card is handed over without its video ROM, which is useless without a screen; with it, the VM’s BIOS froze at boot.
  • Driver: Debian’s (non-free, rebuilt by DKMS for every kernel, fixed by Debian updates) rather than NVIDIA’s.
  • Containers: NVIDIA’s tools, from their repository (key stored in the Ansible role, fingerprint checked), in CDI mode. A container requests nvidia.com/gpu=all, Docker’s configuration stays untouched, and the card’s description is regenerated at every boot, before Docker.
  • Applications: Immich runs recognition with CUDA and converts videos on the card (NVENC, NVDEC).

Consequences

  • Measured on a 4K HDR film: decoding at 157 frames/s, 4K HDR → 1080p conversion at 170 frames/s, i.e. 7 times real time. At rest, the card stays around 33 °C with its fan stopped.
  • Dependency on the driver: without the card or its driver, the containers that request it no longer start. A probe (presence, temperature, load) alerts if the card disappears, for example after a new kernel, or if it runs hot. A degraded mode, which removes the card request, brings everything back to the processor.
  • Hardware checked at boot: if the card is removed or replaced, the VM refuses to start until the mapping is removed.
  • Memory: the VM’s memory is reserved in full on the host, which direct memory access by the card requires.
  • Card limits: 2 GB of graphics memory, no AV1, at most 8 simultaneous encodes on a GeForce. NVIDIA drops Pascal after its 580 branch, to be revisited when moving to Debian 14.
