Post-mortem: the WAF ran out of memory, then out of disk

A phone's automatic backup got the WAF killed 139 times in one night; every kill left an upload file behind, until the disk filled up and large responses failed on every site.

12 October 2026 #incident#waf#modsecurity#memory#storage 3 min read

Summary

ImpactFrom 11/10 around 9:20 pm to 13/10 at 10:25 pm: photo and video uploads from the phone interrupted, then, from the morning of 12/10, large responses failing on the published sites (lists, thumbnails, video playback)
Services affectedThe drive and photos first, then anything behind the WAF as soon as a response exceeds what nginx keeps in memory
Causes1) one upload path of the drive still inspected, so loaded whole into memory; 2) the temporary files of interrupted uploads never deleted
DetectionProcessusTuesFauteDeMemoire alert at the first kill (9:25 pm), but only as a warning; critical EspaceDisqueCritique alert 9.5 hours later; users reported it on 13/10

Context

After the 30/09 incident, the photo upload paths and the drive’s chunked uploads no longer go through ModSecurity or AppSec: both modules load the whole request body into memory to inspect it. The drive’s Android sync app, however, sends part of the files in one piece, with a PUT request on another path that was still inspected.

Root causes

Memory. The 30/09 fix was written from the paths seen that day: chunked uploads from the sync clients and uploads from the photo gallery. The Android app’s single-piece uploads (files below the chunking threshold) go through /remote.php/dav/files/, which was still inspected. Every video was loaded whole into memory, once per concurrent request.

Disk. nginx writes an upload’s body to a temporary file, which it deletes when the request ends. A process killed by the kernel deletes nothing: each kill left a 40 to 80 MB file behind. In one night, 11.5 GB on a 16 GB disk. Once full, no large response could get through: the outage spread to services that had nothing to do with uploads.

Detection. The alert on killed processes worked from the first kill, but as a warning: it only shows on the dashboard. The first notification came from the disk, nine and a half hours later, and the fix waited for users to report it.

What went well / less well

  • Well: the centralised logs tied together, within minutes, the nginx kills, the uploads behind them and the abandoned files; no data lost (the originals stay on the phone, which uploads again).
  • Less well: a fix limited to the paths seen on 30/09, with no inventory of each application’s upload paths; the WAF still had 2 GB of memory, although 3 GB had been planned since 30/09.

Corrective actions

  • /remote.php/dav/files/ added to the drive’s upload paths: neither ModSecurity nor AppSec. Still applied: country filtering, rate limiting, CrowdSec bans and the application’s own authentication.
  • Hourly cleanup of abandoned upload files: older than 2 hours, open by no process.
  • WAF VM memory raised to 3 GB.
  • Notify on processes killed for lack of memory, instead of only showing them: decision pending.
  • Rule adopted: every path through which an application uploads files is declared as an upload path, checked in its access logs, not only in its documentation.
