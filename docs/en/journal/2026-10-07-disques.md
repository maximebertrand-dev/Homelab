# A fifth disk, then a disk replaced

## Burn in before trusting

Two second-hand SAS disks arrived: one to grow the pool, the other as a spare. The first only joined the pool after a
SMART long test, then a write and read-back of its whole surface (badblocks): zero bad blocks. Along the way, the
burn-in revealed that **the write cache was disabled on every disk**: writes topped out at 23 MB/s. It is now enabled
by a system rule, set so that the flushes requested by ZFS are actually passed to the disks.

## RAIDZ2 from 4 to 5 disks, without downtime

ZFS can now grow a RAIDZ by adding a disk to it. The expansion copied 8.5 TB in 16 hours, with the pool online and
redundant, then a full scrub found no errors: 13.6 TB raw instead of 10.9. Data already written keeps its old layout
until it is rewritten: still to do, without the snapshots that would double the space used.

## A disk wearing out

One of the original disks had a growing list of bad sectors: 2, then 3 during the expansion, then 4. It was replaced
by the spare. With no free caddy for the server, it had to be taken offline before the swap: during the rebuild, the
pool could only survive one more failure instead of two. The disk was located by its SAS address in the remote
management card (which does not show serial numbers), its bay checked against the label, then 1.65 TB rebuilt in
14 h 40, without errors.

Two lessons: keep a free caddy ready, to replace a disk without reducing redundancy; and silence the "pool degraded"
alert for the planned duration of an intervention, instead of letting it ring every six hours.
